ABeamline, storage ring to chamber beam order, folded into three rows; optical path straightened (the real beam is deflected vertically at M101, the mono and M121)

A′What each stage is and does one card per element: what it is · what moving it does · one line per motor with its range (lv-config) and typical value (panel 2026-08-31, simulator start)

Storage ring§A.1
The ALS ring at 1.9 GeV; electrons circulating in it make the X-rays.
Beam Current is the ring current; Beam Dumped goes on when it falls below 10 mA. It is only logged: a scan that spans a dump is discarded and the optics realigned, no automatic resume (Thomas, 2026-09-09).
Beam Current ~500 mA, topped up · 0 when the beam is down
EPU§A.2
The undulator: two rows of magnets the electron beam wiggles through.
The gap sets the photon energy, shifting the rows (Z) turns in-plane into circular polarization; operators pick polarization 100 or 190 and never move gap or Z by hand.
EPU Gap unbounded (mm) · typ 0 on the panel
EPU Z unbounded (mm)
M101§A.3
The first mirror after the undulator.
Its mount drifts with heat, so the two deflections are jogged by hand during beamtime to recover intensity; no automation has worked. M101 Feedback does not exist (a 25-year-old attempt, Thomas 2026-09-09).
M101 Vertical Deflection unbounded (mrad) · typ −11.1
M101 Horizontal Deflection unbounded (mrad) · typ 2.3
Mono 101§A.4
One mirror carrying three gratings (250, 500, 1000 lines/mm) that selects the photon energy.
Beamline Energy is the number scanned and the record a CCD Scan writes (a Beamline Scan writes Energy Goal instead, Thomas 2026-09-09): LabVIEW looks it up in a table and moves the EPU gap and the mono together; Mono 101 Vessel picks the grating and Energy Type (a state variable) picks the table, once or twice a day.
Beamline Energy unbounded (eV) · typ 270 eV on the panel · 200–1500 usable
Mono 101 Vessel unbounded (mm)
Exit slits§A.5
Two blade pairs at the focus of the upstream optics, downstream of M103 (Thomas, 2026-09-09); that focus is what gets imaged onto the sample.
Size opens the blades symmetrically, Position shifts both; the vertical size sets the resolution. The micron readouts are not physical (62 means about 25 µm).
Horizontal Exit Slit Size unbounded (µm) · typ 2000
Vertical Exit Slit Size unbounded (µm) · typ 62 (≈ 25 µm real)
M103§A.6
The Kirkpatrick–Baez focusing pair, between the mono and the exit slits (Thomas, 2026-09-09).
Yaw moves the beam, Bend Up / Bend Down move the focal point; set once per beamtime to encoded presets, no live optimisation.
M103 Yaw unbounded (mm) · typ 2.79
M103 Bend Up unbounded (mm) · typ 0.57
M103 Bend Down unbounded (mm) · typ 1.02
PZT shutter§A.7
The old piezo shutter, now used only by the PEEM instrument that shares the beamline.
Its translation is sent to a stored position to park it out of the beam when this endstation takes over.
PiezoShutter Trans unbounded (mm) · typ −10.0 (parked)
M121§A.8
The vertical mirror that hands the beam to this endstation.
Two positions only, in or out; drive until the motor limit. The panel's To REFL button is this handover.
M121 Translation unbounded (mm) · typ 14.7 (in)
Diag 106§A.9
A diagnostic paddle right after M121 carrying a spare gold-mesh I0.
Moved in about once a decade; because it is normally out, its Izero signal reads nonsense.
Diag 106 unbounded (mm) · typ 95.0 (out)
Izero 10 nA/V
Higher Order Suppressor§A.10
Grazing mirrors that strip the undulator's higher harmonics.
Moving it up attenuates the beam roughly linearly, which reflectivity uses to keep the detector in range.
Higher Order Suppressor −6 … 13 mm · typ 6.0
Gold mesh§A.11
A gold-coated copper mesh permanently in the beam before all the slits.
The beam knocks electrons out of the gold; the current flowing back (AI 3 Izero) is proportional to intensity, costs about 10 % of the beam, and normalises every measurement. Reads negative.
AI 3 Izero 1 nA/V · about −0.02 V on the panel
Shutters§A.12
A 2 ms fast shutter that times exposures, and one element downstream the old air shutter, kept as an emergency stop.
They sit after the gold mesh, before the scatter slits (Thomas, 2026-09-09). The camera opens and closes the fast shutter around every exposure; Shutter Inhibit (a software flag) stops it from opening, which is how a dark image is taken.
fast 2 ms per cycle · air 100–120 ms, left open
Scatter slits (JJ)§A.13
Three sets (upstream, middle, in-chamber) of horizontal and vertical blade pairs; JJ is the maker.
Aperture moves a pair's two blades symmetrically to set the beam size, Trans moves both to set its position; aligned in order upstream → middle → in-chamber; hard limits, nothing can be damaged. All twelve read in mm (Thomas, 2026-09-09).
12 motors, no software limits · all read 0 on 2026-08-31 (encoders unplugged)
Sample stage§B.2
A four-axis manipulator hanging from the back wall at pipe height: X slides the whole stack along the door axis, Y and Z form a cross on the rotation stage Θ, the sample bar bolts on top.
X brings the bar into the beam (out below 8 mm); Θ turns the bar from grazing (0°) to normal incidence (90°); Y and Z swap between along-the-beam and up/down as Θ turns. TEY is the drain current from the isolated sample plate.
Sample X 5 … 50 mm · typ parked 25 in the sim · 0 on the panel
Sample Y 0 … 41 mm · typ 0 on the panel
Sample Z −5 … 5 mm · typ 0
Sample Theta −181 … 190 ° · typ 0
TEY 100 pA/V
Detector§B.3
The 2D camera (AXIS-SXR-40, bare sCMOS sensor) on an arc, a sideways slide and an in/out slide; the plate on top of its face (the forehead) is the part that collides.
CCD Θ swings the whole camera up through the scattering angles (−6° stop: the PEEM pass-through pipe; +122° stop: the in-chamber slits); CCD X moves it sideways (growing toward the back wall) so the spot lands elsewhere on the sensor, or at 90 puts the door-side photodiode in the beam; CCD Y sets the distance, hence the angles that fit on the sensor. CCD Y is motor counts between two hard stops, not a distance; presets BS Center / BS Edge put the beam stop at the sensor centre or its edge (Thomas, 2026-09-09).
CCD Theta −6 … 122 ° · typ 0
CCD X −100 … 95 mm · typ −10.2 · 90 = Photodiode Far
CCD Y 0 … 80.05 mm · typ parked 80 (safe)
Beam stop§B.4
A small block on the camera body in front of the sensor.
Hides the direct beam, which would saturate the sensor; because it rides on the camera, a CCD X move needs the opposite Beam Stop X move to keep the beam covered. Its motors are the SmarAct X and Y axes, only Y in use and parked until its encoders are shielded (Thomas, 2026-09-09). Next cycle it becomes a photodiode (AI 6).
Beam Stop X −4 … 19.5 mm · typ −1.7
Beam Stop Y −20 … 20 µm · typ 0
AI 6 BeamStop 100 nA/V
Photodiode§B.5
The calibrated photodiode on the door side of the camera body (Thomas, 2026-09-09).
The everyday diagnostic: the Photodiode Far preset slides the camera aside along CCD X so the diode sits in the beam.
Photodiode 1 nA/V · reads negative · preset Photodiode Far = CCD Y 80 → Θ 0 → X 90
Ring lamp and video camera§B.6
A ring light and a video camera outside the vacuum, looking through a viewport at the top of the chamber right above the in-chamber slits, in the beam direction (Thomas, 2026-09-09).
The lamp lights the chamber so the camera can find the sample (YAG for the beam, crosshair for its position); it must be off during any exposure or the bare sensor records background and blobs. The detector cannot hit them.
Light Output on/off · panel button “Light in Chamber”

A″Operating rules and glossary what the scientist does every time, from bl11012/bl11012_knowledge.md §A.4, §A.11, §A.12, §B.6, §B.7, §C.5; words from §C.6

Before any acquisition
Light Output off (the lamp puts background and blobs on the bare sensor), Air Shutter Output on (open), Shutter Inhibit off. The queueserver plans switch the light off themselves.
Dark image
Shutter Inhibit high, then count: the camera runs the exposure without ever opening the fast shutter.
Moving the detector
Out, then over: CCD Y → 80 first, then Sample X or CCD Θ, never a diagonal move. Large θ angles only at CCD Y = 80. LabVIEW does not enforce this; the simulator refuses the move.
Scanning energy
Scan Beamline Energy (the record a CCD Scan writes). Never change Mono 101 Vessel or Energy Type during a scan: a grating change moves the beam and needs realignment.
Normalising
Divide every frame by the average of AI 3 Izero over its exposure; it is the only monitor that is always in the beam. Izero (the paddle) is normally out and reads nonsense.
Beam dump
Beam Dumped is only logged. A scan that spans a dump is discarded and the optics are realigned before continuing; no automatic resume.
Signs and gains
The real current channels read negative. Keithley gains (Photodiode 1 nA/V, Izero 10 nA/V, AI 3 1 nA/V, TEY 100 pA/V, AI 6 100 nA/V) are not yet remotely adjustable; LabVIEW auto-scales only the photodiode.
Finding the sample
Ring light on, video camera: the YAG shows the beam, the crosshair marks it; move Sample X in (nothing is in the beam below 8 mm) and Sample Y along the rows. Then light off.

Glossary

EPU Elliptically Polarizing Undulator; shifting its magnet rows (Z) turns in-plane into circular polarization.
Mono Monochromator: one mirror with three selectable line gratings (250 / 500 / 1000 lines per mm).
KB mirrors Kirkpatrick–Baez focusing mirror pair (M103).
HOS Higher Order Suppressor: grazing mirrors that attenuate higher harmonics; used for reflectivity.
I0 / Izero Beam-intensity monitor (gold mesh). Two here: the permanent upstream mesh AI 3 Izero and the rarely used paddle Izero.
TEY Total Electron Yield: absorption measured as the drain current replenishing photo-ejected electrons.
Scatter slits (JJ) Slit sets whose two blade pairs shape the beam; JJ is the manufacturer, kept in the LabVIEW names. Aperture = opening, Trans = position.
PEEM The other instrument sharing this beamline; runs before this endstation each week and still uses the old PZT shutter.
Keithley The electrometers in the hutch corner reading every real analog input over BNC.
Derived element A LabVIEW number (motor position, DIO state) exposed as a pseudo-AI so it gets logged in the per-step scan file.
Trajectory A stored, ordered multi-motor move to a standard safe position (CCD Positions tab).
Door axis The axis of the chamber's big door: horizontal, roughly perpendicular to the beam; the rotation axis of Sample Θ and CCD Θ and the direction of the three X motors.
Forehead The plate on top of the detector face, the part that collides with the sample stack.
YAG Scintillator screen the video camera uses to find the beam.
RSOXS / XRR Resonant soft X-ray scattering (transmission through SiN windows) / X-ray reflectivity (grazing incidence on Si coupons).
d. The devices container in Bluesky: d = load_epics_devices("SIM11012:"); d.motors["sample_x"] is one ophyd device.

BChamber, side elevation seen from the door · the vertical scattering plane · photo from the same spot beside it · hover the parts

  • Viewpoint. You stand at the chamber door looking in, exactly where the photo was taken: the beam comes from the right, the detector is on the left, downstream. The door axis points at you.
  • Sample axes at θ = 0 (bar horizontal, surface parallel to the beam, confirmed 2026-09-09): X ⊙ along the door axis, 5–50 mm, inboard–outboard (sign to come from Thomas) · Y along the beam, 0–41 mm · Z up/down, ±5 mm · Θ −181 … 190° about X. At θ = 90° the bar stands up (transmission): Y becomes up/down and Z runs along the beam (bl11012/bl11012_knowledge.md §B.1).
  • Detector. The goniometer sits outside the chamber; two beams with an L bracket carry CCD X and, on top, CCD Y, and the whole elbow rotates with θ. CCD Θ rises toward the top of the chamber (confirmed 2026-09-09); its −6° stop keeps it off the steel pass-through pipe below the beam, its +122° stop off the in-chamber slits. CCD Y is motor counts between two hard stops (0 in, 80 out and safe); large θ angles are used only at CCD Y = 80. CCD X and Beam Stop X run along the door axis and only show on sheet C. PD is the photodiode on the door side of the body; BS the beam stop riding on the camera in front of the sensor (SmarAct motors; Beam Stop Y in mm; AI 6 BeamStop replaces it next cycle). The body moves toward the sample along CCD Y (0 at the sample, 80 fully out, no visible rail: it travels on the θ arm) and rides an X rail into the page (10 near you, 90 at the back wall); parked at 80 its back is almost against the downstream wall. The dark lip on top of its face is the forehead, the part that hits the sample stand when CCD Y → 0 with Sample X near 50.
  • Sample stand. The Y/Z/Θ stack hangs under the bar at about the height of the pass-through pipe, mounted from the back wall rather than standing on the floor: Z stage on top, Y motor along the beam on the upstream side, θ stepper behind, the Sample X slide at the base. The steel pipe runs just under the detector's lowest reach; the −6° stop exists so the detector never touches it.
  • Light and camera. The ring light (Light Output) and the video camera are outside the vacuum, at a viewport on top of the chamber right above the in-chamber slits, looking in the beam direction at a slight angle (Thomas, 2026-09-09). A YAG finds the beam, a crosshair marks it, samples sit at the camera's focus. The detector cannot hit them.

CChamber, plan top view · as if you were flying above the chamber looking down at its floor · door at the bottom · hover the parts

  • Top view, door at the bottom. Imagine flying above the chamber and looking straight down at its floor, with the door nearest you: back wall and feedthroughs far away at the top, beam from the right. The Y / Z / Θ stack hangs directly under the sample bar (mounted from the back wall, not on the floor); only its Sample X slide runs along the door axis (dashed rail), so the whole footprint moves toward the door as X grows (sign still to come from Thomas). CCD X grows away from the door (his sketch: 10 at the door side, 90 at the back), which is how the Photodiode Far preset brings the door-side diode into the beam; Beam Stop X runs along the same axis; Sample Θ and CCD Θ rotate about it.
  • Collision. With the stack slid to X ≈ 50 and the detector brought in (CCD Y ≤ 20) the forehead plate on the detector face meets the Y/Z motor stack (hatch). Move out (CCD Y → 80), then over, never diagonally (bl11012/bl11012_knowledge.md §B.7). Thomas (2026-09-09): this is the only real collision pair, everything is risky at CCD Y → 0, and LabVIEW does not enforce it; the simulation refuses the move, so it is stricter than the beamline. The zone numbers are placeholders.
  • Photodiode Far. CCD Y 80 → CCD Θ 0 → CCD X 90 slides the body along the door axis until the door-side photodiode sits in the beam.

Relationships the simulator enforces

  • Beamline Energy drives Beamline Energy Goal, Mono Energy (same number) and EPU Gap (lookup table); the move completes when the slowest follower lands (registry: Beamline Energy drives the EPU gap and the mono).
  • EPU Polarization drives EPU Z through a table (100 → 0 mm, 190 → 16 mm, placeholders).
  • Sample X / CCD Y collision interlock: a move of Sample X into 45–50 while CCD Y is in 0–20, or the reverse, is refused like a limit violation, including moves still in flight (zones are placeholders; the real LabVIEW does not enforce this).
  • The camera-setting motors Camera ROI X/Y/Width/Height, X/Y Bin and CCD Camera Shutter Inhibit mirror the detector record `sim_det` and the `shutter_inhibit` DIO.
  • The detector's frames are dark unless Shutter Output and Air Shutter Output are on and Shutter Inhibit is off; brightness scales with Beam Current and the Higher Order Suppressor; Light Output adds background; the spot follows CCD X / CCD Θ, its size CCD Y, the shadow Beam Stop X, the sample Sample X.
  • The camera pulses Shutter Output around every exposure; `count([sim_det])` is bright out of the box and Shutter Inhibit high gives a dark frame.

Answered on 2026-09-09

  • Arc rises toward the top of the chamber; the −6° hard stop is the steel pass-through pipe to the PEEM chamber, the +122° stop protects the in-chamber slits (JJ). Both stops are physical, on the goniometer outside the chamber.
  • Ring light and video camera are outside the vacuum, at a viewport on top of the chamber above the in-chamber slits, looking downstream at the sample. The detector cannot hit them.
  • Standing at the door, the beam comes from the right; the door axis is only roughly perpendicular to the beam. Everything else on sheet B is right.
  • Photodiode on the door side of the camera body. Sample θ = 0 is horizontal, 90° is transmission.
  • From Thomas's annotated photos: CCD X grows away from the door (10 at the door side, 90 at the back wall), CCD Y counts 0 at the sample and 80 fully out; the θ arm from the back-wall feedthrough carries the detector; the stack is X at the base, θ at the back, Z on top, Y along the beam.
  • Order along the beam: mono → M103 → exit slits → PZT shutter → M121 → Diag 106 → HOS → gold mesh → shutters → scatter slits.
  • M101 Feedback does not exist. Entrance slit width is in microns; the eV entry is a leftover. The four Vertical/Horizontal Slit Position/Size motors are exit-slit axes after M103.
  • Beam Dumped is only logged; no pause-and-resume after a dump (the optics must be realigned).
  • CCD Y is motor counts between two hard stops (0 and 80); the 100mm / 0mm presets are legacy; BS Center = beam stop at the sensor centre, BS Edge = CCD X moved so the stop sits at the edge for more q range.
  • T-2T couples sample θ and CCD Θ (5° → 10°); the offset corrects non-coaxial axes; both unused.
  • Controllers: no answer until Kevin finishes the 6K → Galil swap; Pollux unknown; SmarAct X/Y are the beam-stop motors, only Y used, inactive until its encoders are shielded.
  • All units are mm (Beam Stop Y, all scatter-slit axes). The slit encoders are cut off, not needed.
  • Only Sample X / CCD Y collide; everything is risky at CCD Y → 0, so large θ only at CCD Y = 80. LabVIEW does not enforce it; the simulator's interlock is stricter than reality.
  • A CCD Scan records Beamline Energy (the right record); a Beamline Scan records Beamline Energy Goal. Energy Type is a state variable, set by hand, never during a scan.
  • Detector temperature is readable through the camera driver's equation. AO 0/1, OSP Adjustment, Sample Number, MCS axes are legacy.
  • Keithley gains must become remotely adjustable; LabVIEW auto-scales only the photodiode gain when it saturates, and only in single-motor scans.
